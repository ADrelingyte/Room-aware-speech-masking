01 / OVERVIEW

Mask the speech.
Model the room.

Can a learned additive masker reduce objective speech intelligibility more effectively than conventional speech-shaped noise at the same energy budget?

Abstract

Open-plan offices present a significant challenge for cognitive performance, as background speech from nearby conversations is a major source of distraction. Noise-generation machines (NGMs) address this by adding masking noise to reduce speech intelligibility, but conventional maskers such as white noise (WN) and speech-shaped noise (SSN) provide limited reduction for a given energy budget. We investigate learned additive maskers in a physically realistic scenario incorporating room impulse responses (RIRs), modeling the NGM as a loudspeaker-based device across three acoustic paths: speaker-to-NGM, speaker-to-listener, and NGM-to-listener. A Wave-U-Net concealer is trained under two objectives: a STOI-only loss and a joint CTC+STOI loss, both subject to a hard mixture-to-concealer ratio (MCR) constraint. Room impulse responses are simulated across randomised office geometries, and the concealer is trained on dry speech convolved with the speaker-to-NGM path. We evaluate on a held-out test set under both anechoic and realistic office-babble conditions across three MCR levels. The results demonstrate that RIR-aware learned maskers substantially degrade objective intelligibility metrics compared with conventional SSN at matched MCRs.

Objective metrics and ASR evaluations only; no human listening-test claim is made.

01

Consistent ASR degradation

At 5 dB MCR, both learned methods yield higher WER than SSN and white noise in both evaluated recognisers and both background conditions.

02

Independent intelligibility metric

At 5 dB MCR, CTC+STOI achieves lower HASPI than both conventional baselines; the WER differences between the two learned objectives are small.

03

Acoustic and delay robustness

The study examines varying reverberation, source distance, and an unseen additional 2.9 ms processing-delay shift.
